The satnav says “left”, but there's a “No entry” sign: which one to trust

Signs come before the satnav. If you see an R-101 “No entry” sign, don't turn, even if your route goes that way.

By ProntoTest

You're in an unfamiliar city, and the satnav tells you to turn left in 50 metres. There's an R-101 “No entry” (Entrada prohibida) sign on the corner, and cars on that street are coming towards you. The satnav keeps insisting, and the cars behind are right on your tail.

What to notice

  • Satnav maps are updated late: the street's traffic layout may have been changed (new signs put up, the street made one-way or pedestrian-only), and the satnav doesn't know about it yet.
  • Drivers on that street aren't expecting an oncoming car, and they pull out from behind parked cars without looking your way.
  • Pedestrians on a one-way street look only in the direction the traffic comes from and may step out right in front of you.

What to do

  1. 1Go where the signs allow, straight on or into another turning; the satnav will recalculate the route by itself.
  2. 2The satnav isn't the law, and the rules require you to obey signs, even when they seem to contradict your route.
  3. 3The traffic officer comes first, then temporary signs and barriers (at roadworks, for example), then traffic lights, ordinary signs and road markings.
  4. 4The R-101 “No entry” sign closes the entry from your side to all vehicles, and turning in past it and then driving against the flow of traffic is a very serious offence: 500 euros and 6 points.
  5. 5If you miss the turn, don't reverse back across the junction: drive round the block.
  6. 6And don't reset the satnav by hand while you're driving: that's another 200 euros and 3 points.
