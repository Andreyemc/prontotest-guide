A car on the roundabout is indicating right: can you enter?

Only enter the roundabout after giving way to everyone already going round it. A right indicator on a car on the roundabout doesn't necessarily mean it will leave in front of you.

By ProntoTest

Top view: an orange car at the entry to a roundabout, with “Roundabout” and “Give way” signs on the right, and a turquoise car going round the ring with its indicator on

You're approaching a roundabout, with signs R-402 “Roundabout” (Sentido obligatorio en glorieta) and R-1 “Give way” (Ceda el paso) at the entry. From the left, a car is coming round the ring towards your entry with its right indicator on: it looks as if it's about to leave right in front of you. Cars are already waiting behind you, and you're tempted to go straight away.

What to notice

  • The driver may have forgotten to switch the indicator off after coming onto the roundabout, or changed their mind and is going on to the next exit.
  • A driver on the roundabout doesn't have to brake for you: they have right of way, even if they're still half a lap away from you.

What to do

  1. 1Approach the roundabout at a speed that lets you stop before the line.
  2. 2Look left: right of way belongs to everyone already going round the roundabout.
  3. 3Don't trust the indicator alone.
  4. 4Wait until the car actually starts to leave: its wheels have turned and it's heading for the exit.
  5. 5If in doubt, let it go by and enter after it.
  6. 6On the roundabout itself, signal your exit early with your right indicator and switch it off as soon as you've left: that makes it easier for drivers waiting at the entry to see where you're going.
  7. 7If there's a working traffic light or a traffic officer at the entry, follow their signals.
